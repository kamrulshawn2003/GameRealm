How Games Tell Stories: Three Paradigms of Interactive Narrative

📅 2026-08-28 · ✍️ Research team · 🏷️ Narrative · ⏱️ 12 min read

Film tells a story in two hours; games hand 'telling stories' to the player...

Paradigm 1: Embedded narrative

Closest to film: the director writes the story; the player merely 'plays' the role.

  • Examples: The Last of Us, Detroit: Become Human.
  • Pros: dense storytelling, strong emotional impact, cinematic.
  • Cost: 'freedom' is often fake — the skeleton doesn't change.

Paradigm 2: Environmental narrative

No cutscenes — the story is scattered in the world: a dropped letter, a ransacked room...

The best game narrative makes you realize: the world was running long before you arrived.
  • Examples: Elden Ring, Darkest Dungeon, RDR2.
  • Pros: respects interpretation; exploration becomes story.
  • Cost: to players who skip item text, the story 'doesn't exist'.

Paradigm 3: Emergent narrative

The most radical: no specific story, only rules and situations — the 'story' emerges from player action.

DimensionEmbeddedEnvironmentalEmergent
Source of storyWriterScene fragmentsPlayer action
Typical length8–20 hrs30–100 hrsEndless
ExamplesThe Last of UsElden RingRimWorld, Minecraft
  • Examples: RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress, Minecraft.
  • Pros: every run is a unique story.
  • Cost: high barrier; the player must assign meaning.

Conclusion: no best, only fitting

The three aren't ranked. Want to be moved? Pick embedded. Want to discover a world? Pick environmental. Want to author your own legend? Pick emergent.
