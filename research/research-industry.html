Gaming Industry 2026: The Premium Revival & Going Global

📅 2026-08-15 · ✍️ Research team · 🏷️ Industry · 👁️ 11 min read

Over recent years the global games industry quietly pivoted: buy-to-play single-player — once thought unprofitable — is back in favor...

1. The single-player revival: from fringe to mainstream

A decade ago many declared 'single-player is dead' — high cost, short life, no live-service revenue. But Elden Ring and Black Myth: Wukong proved:

When quality is high enough, players still pay full price for a complete, one-time story.
  • Player fatigue: exhaustion with endless grind, gacha and power creep.
  • Media & streamers: premium games naturally travel as content.
  • Mature tech: engines and middleware lower AAA barriers.

2. Chinese studios going global: from 'reskin' to 'brand'

Chinese games abroad went through three stages:

  • Early 2010s — The tooling phase: Publishing, user-acquisition and minor tweaks; products looked alike.
  • 2018–2022 — The product phase: Genshin Impact, PUBG Mobile begin influencing global markets with original IP.
  • 2023–now — The brand phase: Moving from product to cultural/aesthetic output; 'made in China' is being re-read worldwide.

3. Market structure shifts

TrendSigns
Platform convergenceConsole/PC/mobile blur; cross-platform play is standard
Subscription riseGame Pass, PS Plus reshape how we 'buy' games
Indie boomSteam and consoles let tiny teams reach the world
AI in the pipelineAI tools enter art, NPCs and localization

4. Risks and caveats

Boom isn't without shadows: rising UA costs, ever-bigger AAA projects, squeezed small teams. Balancing commerce and artistic expression is a question every studio must answer.

Conclusion

For players, this is the best era: we have industrial peaks like Elden Ring and surprises from two-person indie teams. As the pendulum swings, those who make and play games seriously benefit in the end.
